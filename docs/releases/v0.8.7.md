# Plainva 0.8.7

One account, one sign-in. This release brings together the changes since 0.8.6: every account signs in once for files, calendar and mail and has a page of its own, Plainva brings its own Google sign-in, one assistant connects every provider, the settings can be searched, queries from the Obsidian Tasks plugin show as task lists, checkbox tasks with a time can remind you, and a long round of fixes for sync, mail, typed input, properties, `.base` files and the phone. Shared improvements apply to desktop and mobile.

If you use Plainva on more than one device, please update all of them (see *Accounts and sign-in* below).

## Accounts and sign-in

- **One sign-in per account.** Connecting a Google or Microsoft account, switching a service on at its card and **Sign in again** ask once for everything the account carries — files, calendar and mail — and each service then says whether it was granted. A service you left unticked on the provider's consent screen is named as such, instead of failing quietly. Connecting an account the vault already has extends it instead of adding a second card.
- **Plainva's own Google sign-in.** Google now works with the **Standard sign-in**: you enter nothing and need no Google project of your own. An **Own client ID** stays possible under **Sign-in on this device**, and the editor now says which client type each device needs (a Desktop-type client on the desktop, an iOS-type client on the phone) and explains “redirect_uri_mismatch”. Thanks for [#142](https://github.com/plainva/plainva/issues/142). Google has not verified Plainva's sign-in yet, so its consent screen can show a notice about an unverified app until it has.
- **A Google account connected through Plainva's earlier sign-in signs in once more.** Plainva's first Google project is retired, and Google refuses its clients. A device that used it — the built-in sign-in on Android, or a client from that project entered as your own — moves to the **Standard sign-in** the first time it reads the setting; the account then asks to be signed in again. A client of any other project stays your choice.
- **One account page for every provider** — Google, Microsoft, Dropbox, iCloud, WebDAV, S3, IMAP and the calendars on this device: one state with at most one button, a switch and a page per service (the cloud folder and interval, the calendars and task lists with their colours, the mail folders, senders and signature). **Check connection** asks each service with what this device holds and says what works, what lapsed and what was never allowed, with an action that helps. **Sign out on this device** removes this device's sign-in and leaves the account, its settings and your other devices alone; **Remove account** asks first and names what goes.
- **A service switched off reaches every device.** Switching files off for an account stops them for the vault on every device; the settings keep travelling, so switching back on reaches the others too. Removing a mailbox and connecting it again are now ordered across devices by a counter instead of by the clocks of the devices. A device on 0.8.6 does not know either, which is why every device should be updated.
- A calendar or mailbox that still signed in on its own hands its sign-in to its account when the vault opens; one that cannot keeps working as before and tries again next time.
- Desktop: a calendar connected together with files no longer fails with “the target account or vault changed”, and the calendar connects on the first sign-in. **Open an online vault** signs in like every other connection and ends with an account card.
- An account added again after a removal is no longer dropped on the next import, and merging duplicate account cards only happens where no mailbox or sign-in is lost; what cannot be merged is offered for repair.

## Connecting

- **One assistant for every provider.** Dropbox, Nextcloud, iCloud, WebDAV, S3 and the IMAP suites sign in through the same step as Google and Microsoft: it says which services it asks for, names each secret as the provider names it and links where to create it, and puts **Other sign-in** (own client ID, Dropbox app key, Gmail with an app password) in one place. Nextcloud has its own entry, and the provider list can be searched.
- **Every service reports its own result.** A failed service no longer stops the run: the others are set up, and each failure says who failed — this device, the provider, the network or Plainva — in one sentence, with the provider's own words underneath where a diagnosis needs them.
- **A step bar for the whole way** — sign in, folder, calendar, email. The folder step asks for the folder with **Choose folder**; every step can be skipped, **Stop** asks first and keeps the sign-in and everything already set up, and a summary ends the run.
- **A folder with notes keeps its own.** The starter notes of a new vault are not copied into a cloud folder that already holds files; an empty folder takes the whole vault.
- The Dropbox app key is a choice per device, in the same place as the Google and Microsoft client.

## Settings

- **Search over all settings**, at the top of the settings rail: a hit names page, card and row, opens the page and lights up the row.
- **Explanations behind the ⓘ.** General explanations moved behind a button at the end of a setting's label; steps and warnings that decide something stay visible. The visible text of the settings is less than half as long as before.
- **Newly ordered pages.** “Content & structure” became **Notes & filing** and **Daily notes & journal**; the OKF commands moved to **Maintenance**; **Updates** is a group of **About & diagnostics**; the **Sync** page carries the sync, with its diagnostics folded at the end; the danger zone of **Security & Sharing** stands at the end of the page. A new **Startup** card holds starting with the system, opening the last vault and restoring the window layout.
- Every on/off setting that acts at once is a switch, and **On this device only** marks a vault setting that does not travel with the vault.
- **The week start belongs to the vault** and travels with it; a vault without one keeps this device's choice.
- A folder or file setting can no longer point into `.plainva`, where notes are not indexed, not synced and get no versions.
- Desktop: the vault has a page of its own, as on the phone — rename it in Plainva (the folder keeps its name), export it as a ZIP, or delete it; a folder Plainva did not create is only forgotten.

## Tasks and calendar

- **Queries from the Tasks plugin.** A ` ```tasks ` block of the Obsidian Tasks plugin shows as a task list in the editor and in reading mode, also inside a quote or callout, with group headings, **Explanation**, rows you can tick and **Edit query**. Lines Plainva does not understand yet are named, not dropped in silence; nothing is ever written into the block.
- **Reminders for checkbox tasks.** A checkbox with a due date and a time — `- [ ] Call Bob 📅 2026-11-10 14:30` — reminds you once **Checkboxes with a time** is switched on for the vault (off by default); a date alone does not remind, and nothing is written into the note. Obsidian's Tasks plugin does not recognise the date of a line with a time after it; Plainva accepts that and says so at the switch.
- **Due dates with quick choices and a time**: Today, Tomorrow, This weekend, Next week and No date, then the month and a time row. “Next week” follows your week start, also when snoozing a mail.
- **A task row does the same everywhere**: a link in a task's title reads as its text and opens its note, subtasks stand under their task, and today's finished tasks fold under **Done (n)**.
- **Deleting an event works the first time.** After an edit the calendar kept the event's old version, so the next delete was refused as “changed remotely” and only a second delete a minute later went through. Every write now keeps the version it got back, and a refused delete looks again before it asks. Thanks for [#119](https://github.com/plainva/plainva/issues/119).
- A repeating Apple reminder ticked in Plainva comes back open with its next date, and its finished copy makes no second note. A write from Plainva no longer erases the notes of an Apple reminder or the description of a CalDAV task.
- An entry placed into a calendar follows its appointment on the phone too, and **All events** deletes a series in a calendar on this device.

## Sync

- **Fewer needless conflict copies.** The base the sync merges against is written in one step, so an app that ends in between no longer leaves it half written; a streamed upload no longer leaves the previous text behind; an image, a PDF or a `.csv` that only got a new stamp in the cloud stays your version and goes up; and a file in another encoding that another program turned back into UTF-8 writes without a copy.
- **“Same name, two files.”** Two files whose names differ only in how a letter is encoded — left in a cloud folder by older versions — are now shown with folder, size and date, and whether they are identical. **Remove duplicates** removes identical copies (their content stays in the version history); **Keep both** gives a differing one a free name such as “Name (2)”. Nothing is removed without your click.
- **Sync data belongs to the folder.** Removing an account no longer clears what the sync knows; connecting the same folder again compares against it instead of making a conflict copy of every changed file. A vault that has synced before never gets a new, empty cloud folder by itself.
- A file the provider refuses for good (Google Drive's “abusive” flag) is skipped and named instead of holding up the vault, and nothing is uploaded over it; your version goes up as a conflict copy, and the open editor follows it.
- A name this device's file system refuses no longer holds the first sync of a vault.
- Notes downloaded by the sync count by when they changed on the server, not by the download.
- Closing a conflict with **Keep both files** or **Delete conflict copy** tells the open editor, and a conflict copy is checked and settled in one step.

## Files and `.base`

- **A `.base` file saved without a change comes back unchanged.** Unknown keys, the keys of each view, view types Plainva does not know (a map, a plugin's board) and Obsidian's own `groupBy` are kept as written. A filter condition Plainva cannot read shows **Not applied** instead of silently showing more rows; `file.folder == ""` means the vault's root, and the `file.tags` column fills. The user guide has a complete `.base` reference.
- **Dot files** such as `.notes.md` or files in `.attachments/` are hidden from every surface — tree, search, graph, suggestions, tags, tasks, databases — the same in both shells and every vault location, and they keep syncing. A link to one still resolves.
- **Pins of the graph and recently opened notes are each device's own.** In a vault folder shared through Dropbox, iCloud or a network share, two devices no longer overwrite each other's pins and recent notes. Obsidian's bookmarks are taken over once each, so a bookmark you removed no longer comes back.
- A note with a pathological paragraph — a line of a hundred thousand brackets, say — no longer holds the app for minutes.
- A line that ends in a carriage return of its own (a note from a classic Mac) is a line for tasks too.

## Writing and editing

- **What you type is kept.** A dialog with typed text — a journal entry, an event, a new folder name, a wizard — asks **Discard your input?** before it closes over it, and so does closing the window or switching the vault.
- A draft is offered only at the note it belongs to: drafts of two notes with similar names, or of two vaults, could share one file and be restored into the wrong note.
- A second window's save that takes long is no longer reported as failed while it still lands, and the quick capture no longer writes its line twice.
- **The OKF conversion never damages its own backup**, converts a note once per run, and its undo puts a note back only while you have not changed it since.
- **List items with several spaces after the marker** keep one gap, and wrapped lines start under the text; a task with spaces before its box no longer draws a bullet too.
- A table cell being edited keeps the system's text menu: a right-click offers spelling and copy. Thanks for [#118](https://github.com/plainva/plainva/issues/118).
- The editor reads pictures, wiki links and the properties block as the reading view does: an encoded picture name such as `Zoom%20one.svg` shows its picture, `[[Note\|alias]]` in a table leads to “Note”, and a value in the properties block no longer unformats the rest of the note. A fence shown inside a code block no longer closes it, and a code block inside a quote or callout is code for every reader — ticking a task in reading view no longer writes into it.
- A task typed anew keeps its box in line with the tasks around it, and a Mermaid diagram in a callout is drawn in the live preview.
- A write you started that fails now says why — a new database, a tick, a template, an adopted overview, a `.base` that could not be saved.

## Links, properties, templates, overviews

- **Only web, mail and phone addresses leave the app.** A link opens outside Plainva only when it is `http`, `https`, `mailto` or `tel`; any other address, from a note, a database cell or a mail, is refused with a notice.
- Properties are read and written by one rule: a block that is not a map is refused by reader and writers alike with one sentence, an alias key is found under its name, and a value keeps its spelling. Keys a note took over from a template (`plainva.tasks: false`, `plainva.templateFor`) are listed in the task view and can be removed after a question.
- Templates: an escaped placeholder stays text, `{{daily}}` follows the day boundary, and **+ Entry** on the desktop follows the folder and type rules. A template assignment follows its database when it moves.
- **Overviews:** adopting a note as a folder's overview takes its bookmarks and open tabs along, counts a `[[#Heading]]` link on its own, and offers to keep links that name another overview only as `index` pointing where they led. Generating an overview offers the same, for one folder and for all at once.
- A new database entry made from an embedded database never replaces a link its host note already carries.

## Mail

- **Every row is its address.** An action — read, star, move, spam, delete, undo, a rule, a snooze — acts on the message it stands on: in a conversation, your reply from Sent could share its number with a message in the inbox, and the action hit that one. Outlook's “Deleted Items” is recognised as the trash in its languages.
- **Snoozing can be undone** from its notice, in **All inboxes** and every list.
- A Microsoft mailbox sends and saves files over 2.8 MB through an upload session.
- A second **File as note** fills a note that was filed without its text, and a picture from the web in a filed mail becomes a link, so opening the note no longer fetches it.
- A message reads the same on both shells: a plain-text mail has a text view on the desktop, other text types and digests open, **+ .eml** works on a note filed earlier, and a `mailto:` link opens Plainva's composer from the message's mailbox.
- The composer and the mail tab notice a mailbox connected or removed while they are open; a busy service says so in plain words; one notice counts every refused message; the desktop list shows each message's opening words.

## Desktop

- The main window starts where you left it instead of centred, and quitting from the tray keeps its place. Settings reach every window.
- A copy installed by a package manager can leave updates to it: with `PLAINVA_UPDATES_MANAGED_BY` set, Plainva does not ask the update feed, and **Updates** says who brings the next version. The Nix flake contributed in [#124](https://github.com/plainva/plainva/pull/124) uses it for its community-maintained `plainva-bin` package (x86_64 Linux) and offers a development shell.

## Phone

The phone has the same changes as above and its own release notes; on its own it gains one-handed writing, a calendar head like every other area, lists for **Recent** and **Bookmarks**, and opening a vault that shows what it is doing instead of a blank screen ([#143](https://github.com/plainva/plainva/issues/143)).

## Security

- Links leave the app only as web, mail or phone addresses (see above); before, the phone handed other schemes from a note to its web view.
- Pictures from the web in filed mails are no longer fetched when the note opens.
- Diagnostics record the address and parameter names of a sign-in answer, never their values.

Encrypted workspaces, the on-device calendar, voice notes and the location stamp remain experimental. These are specific corrections and improvements, not an independent security audit.
